# software-development-requirements

## Activation and inputs

Clarify requirements, scope and acceptance criteria before design. Inputs: User goals, existing product records and constraints. Read applicable central AGENTS.md; this capability grants no additional authorisation.

## Procedure

start with first identifying the most significant decisions to be made and ask users for those decisions while suggesting recommended approaches.

Follow the collaborative discovery rules in software-development-workflow. Interview the user in focused rounds about users, goals, examples, business rules, priorities, constraints and success/failure cases; follow up until material ambiguity is resolved. Brainstorm candidate workflows when useful, then separate confirmed needs from exploratory ideas and seek approval of the detailed proposal.

Read relevant product/code evidence. Separate confirmed needs from assumptions. Assign requirement IDs; define scope/exclusions, stories where useful and observable positive/negative acceptance criteria. Propose priorities and backlog additions without changing existing entries. Resolve conflicts and present a concrete revision for requirements approval.

For `/backlog`, inspect project evidence and research external sources before suggesting features and asking focused questions; cite evidence and disclose limitations. For `/plan`, match the saved backlog first and save new directly requested items before requirements work, if the environment permits. Follow the shared product backlog contract in software-development-workflow; discovery approval is separate from requirements approval.

## Outputs and boundaries

Requirements proposal; criteria; open questions; approval target. Include decisions, deliverable references, evidence, risks/blockers and next action. Feeds software-development-planning and design; does not select architecture.
Before you write any direct response to me, ensure it is written in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, do not use techno-babble of any kind, do not physicalize things, always use the simplest turn of phrase, no need for synonymous phrases for variety, and use active voice.
Be concise and minimalist.
